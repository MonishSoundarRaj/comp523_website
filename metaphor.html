System Metaphor

An XP-style system metaphor. One shared picture of the system that the whole team, our client, and the librarians can all use.

Library Navigator is the reference desk, open at two in the morning.

You walk up and describe what you need in your own words. The person behind the desk knows what this library actually has, not what libraries have in general, so they do not send you to a site that wants a card you were never issued. They do not just point at a shelf either. They walk you over, show you where the login is, and wait while you try it. And when they are not sure, they say so and go ask someone, instead of guessing and sounding confident.

Why this one

We wanted a metaphor our client and the librarians could use without translation, and the reference desk is the thing our system is replacing after hours. It also settles arguments. When we cannot agree whether a feature belongs, the question becomes whether a good reference librarian would do it. A good one does not lecture you about database licensing. A good one does not pretend to know. A good one walks you over.

It keeps us honest about the hard parts too. The reason the assistant refuses to answer at low confidence is not a technical rule we invented. It is what the person at the desk does when they are unsure.

The names it gives us

XP asks that the metaphor carry through into what we call things. These are the words we use in design discussions and in the code.

The desk
The chat interface. Where someone walks up and asks.
The librarian
The AI service. Answers from what this branch holds, and cites it.
The shelf list
The curated dataset in PostgreSQL. What this library actually carries.
Walking you over
Guided step-by-step navigation. One instruction, then wait.
Let me check on that
Low-confidence escalation. No answer is asserted; a human is offered.
Keeping the list
The librarian admin panel, the audit log, and the review queue.
The back office
The server. Patrons never see it, and nothing sensitive leaves it.

See also the architecture diagram, which shows the same pieces as software rather than as people.
